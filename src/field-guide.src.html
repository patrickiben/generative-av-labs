Where this stands

Nebula was tested in Chrome on a Mac with a simulated audio input and a virtual MIDI port, using a synthetic drum loop. No real drum kit has been played through it yet, so its pad map and audio estimates are starting points. Endless Cockpit was tested with a simulated Bluetooth sensor, and no real cadence sensor has been connected yet.

Wiring a drum kit for Nebula

Nebula is the one page here that listens to a drum kit. Any kit with 5-pin or USB MIDI and line outputs works.

  1. Audio. Two 1/4-inch TRS cables from the module's main outputs to two line inputs on your audio interface. If that input shares a front instrument jack, leave the jack empty, because a plug there can take over the input.
  2. MIDI. Many audio interfaces have no MIDI ports, and a module's USB port is not always MIDI. If so, run a 5-pin cable from the module's MIDI OUT into a USB-MIDI interface, and plug that into the computer.
  3. In the page. Press Connect, then Connect audio and MIDI, and allow both prompts. Choose your audio interface for audio. The MIDI list shows your interface's name, not the module's.
  4. Listening. Nebula never plays your input back, so keep monitoring through your interface.

Riding Endless Cockpit

Endless Cockpit takes its speed from your cadence. Connect a Bluetooth cadence sensor or smart trainer from the Connect sheet, hold Space or the Hold to pedal button, or set a steady cadence. Each crank revolution sends a ring of light down the road, and the road's grade changes your speed by up to 40%.

The sensor's status reads not connected, no Bluetooth in this browser, none chosen, connected with no cadence yet, live, or disconnected. Some sensors send nothing until the crank turns.

Nebula: MIDI and audio together

MIDI names the drum, its velocity, the hi-hat pedal and cymbal chokes. Audio gives the picture its real spectrum. When both are live, MIDI decides the hits, so nothing fires twice.

With audio alone, four frequency bands estimate kick, snare, hat, tom and crash by a stated rule, and a ride is not told apart. With MIDI alone, each hit paints a stated spectrum shape rather than a measured one.

The note numbers on your module can be changed, so the map is a starting point. Open Connect, then Pad map and Learn, press Learn beside a pad and hit it.

Chimera Synth

Chimera is the synth here. Your kit or a keyboard plays it over MIDI, and it never listens to an audio input. Tested in Chrome on a Mac with a virtual MIDI port. No real kit has played it yet.

  • Wiring. The same MIDI route as above. Only the MIDI cable matters here. The sound plays through the computer's output, such as your interface's monitors or headphones.
  • What each drum plays. A stated mapping, a musical choice: the kick plays the root an octave down, the snare +2, the side stick +3, the hi-hat +10, or +11 open, the toms -8, -5 and -3 from low to high, crashes +5 and rides +6. The hi-hat pedal (CC4) lets an open hat ring longer, and grabbing a cymbal cuts its lean short. Other notes play as keys, with middle C as the root.
  • Status. Sound reads off, ready, playing, or paused by the browser, which starts sound only after a click. MIDI reads as on the other pages: not connected, waiting, permission denied, no device, open with no notes, live, or disconnected. The demo says the page is playing itself.
  • Files. Samples are decoded in the browser. Chrome and Firefox open WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg but not AIFF or CAF, so convert those first. Safari opens all of them, but has no Web MIDI.

Displacer

Displacer blends two pictures or two sounds from files, so it needs no kit and listens to no input. Tested in Chrome on a Mac, with MIDI fed through the page's own parser. No real controller has driven it yet.

  • The mod wheel. CC1 moves the slider from A at 0 to B at 127, as in the desktop app. Any MIDI controller with a mod wheel works.
  • A MIDI keyboard. With a patch grown and its box ticked, the keyboard plays the patch, with middle C as its root.
  • Files. Decoded in the browser and never uploaded. Chrome opens WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg, and the page reads AIFF itself. CAF is refused with its name. A sound uses its first six seconds.
  • Performance. Perform fills the screen with the stage alone, and Esc leaves. For a projector, drag the window onto it first.

Safety and privacy

A limiter slows any large change in brightness, and these pages measure under the WCAG limit of three flashes a second, even in each page's hardest tested case. That is a measured screen, not a medical guarantee. If flashing or moving light affects you, take care, and turn on your system's reduced-motion setting, which tightens the limiter, calms the camera and removes the flash.

Audio, MIDI and cadence are read in this browser and never sent anywhere. A session file holds hits, pedal moves, cadence and levels, with no audio in it.
