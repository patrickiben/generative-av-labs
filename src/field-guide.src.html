Where this stands

Nebula was tested in Chrome on a Mac with a simulated audio input and a virtual MIDI port, using a synthetic drum loop. No real drum kit has been played through it yet, so its pad map and audio estimates are starting points.

Wiring a drum kit for Nebula

Nebula is the one page here that listens to a drum kit. Any kit with 5-pin or USB MIDI and line outputs works.

  1. Audio. Two 1/4-inch TRS cables from the module's main outputs to two line inputs on your audio interface. If that input shares a front instrument jack, leave the jack empty, because a plug there can take over the input.
  2. MIDI. Many audio interfaces have no MIDI ports, and a module's USB port is not always MIDI. If so, run a 5-pin cable from the module's MIDI OUT into a USB-MIDI interface, and plug that into the computer.
  3. In the page. Press Connect, then Connect audio and MIDI, and allow both prompts. Choose your audio interface for audio. The MIDI list shows your interface's name, not the module's.
  4. Listening. Nebula never plays your input back, so keep monitoring through your interface.

Nebula: MIDI and audio together

MIDI names the drum, its velocity, the hi-hat pedal and cymbal chokes. Audio gives the picture its real spectrum. When both are live, MIDI decides the hits, so nothing fires twice.

With audio alone, four frequency bands estimate kick, snare, hat, tom and crash by a stated rule, and a ride is not told apart. With MIDI alone, each hit paints a stated spectrum shape rather than a measured one.

The note numbers on your module can be changed, so the map is a starting point. Open Connect, then Pad map and Learn, press Learn beside a pad and hit it.

Chimera Synth

Chimera is the synth here. A MIDI keyboard or the computer keys play it, and it never listens to an audio input. Tested in Chrome on a Mac with a virtual MIDI port. No real keyboard has played it yet.

  • Connecting. A USB MIDI keyboard plugs straight in, and a keyboard with a 5-pin MIDI OUT needs a USB-MIDI interface. The sound plays through the computer's output.
  • Notes. Middle C is the root, and every channel plays the same way. From the computer, Z to M and Q to U play two octaves.
  • Status. Sound reads off, ready, playing, or paused by the browser, which starts sound only after a click. MIDI reads not connected, waiting, permission denied, no device, open with no notes, live, or disconnected. The demo says the page is playing itself.
  • Files. Samples are decoded in the browser. Chrome and Firefox open WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg but not AIFF or CAF, so convert those first. Safari opens all of them, but has no Web MIDI.

Detail Forge Analog

Analog makes a dull, band-limited recording brighter and says how. It cannot recover lost audio: Floor amplifies what is there, Exciter and SBR synthesize new detail above the measured cutoff, and Transient lifts attacks with a moving gain that also spreads some new highs above it. Tested in Chrome on a Mac with test files made by ffmpeg. No outside listener has judged its sound yet.

  • Files. WAV and AIFF are read straight from the file. FLAC, MP3, M4A and Ogg are decoded by the browser at the rate in the file's own header, so it does not resample them, and the Source sheet says which happened. Up to 10 minutes and two channels.
  • Range. Drag across the spectrograms to enhance one stretch, with 50 ms crossfades at the seams. The numbers are measured on that stretch, which fixes a window the desktop app measured in the wrong place.
  • Listening. A and B play from the same instant, and Space flips between them. B is scaled by the BS.1770 loudness gain, so brighter is never mistaken for louder.
  • Status. The source badge reads synthetic test sound or your file's name. The processing chip names the engine and whether it amplified or synthesized, and the sound chip reads off, playing A, playing B or stopped.

Displacer

Displacer blends two sounds from files, so it needs no kit and listens to no input. Tested in Chrome on a Mac, with MIDI fed through the page's own parser. No real controller has driven it yet.

  • The mod wheel. CC1 moves the slider from A at 0 to B at 127, as in the desktop app. Any MIDI controller with a mod wheel works.
  • A MIDI keyboard. With a patch grown and its box ticked, the keyboard plays the patch, with middle C as its root.
  • Files. Decoded in the browser and never uploaded. Chrome opens WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg, and the page reads AIFF itself. CAF is refused with its name. A sound uses its first six seconds.
  • Performance. Perform fills the screen with the stage alone, and Esc leaves. For a projector, drag the window onto it first.

Safety and privacy

A limiter slows any large change in brightness, and these pages measure under the WCAG limit of three flashes a second, even in each page's hardest tested case. That is a measured screen, not a medical guarantee. If flashing or moving light affects you, take care, and turn on your system's reduced-motion setting, which tightens the limiter, calms the camera and removes the flash.

Audio and MIDI are read in this browser and never sent anywhere. A session file holds hits, pedal moves and levels, with no audio in it.
