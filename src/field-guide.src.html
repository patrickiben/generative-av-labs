Field guide

From the kit to the light.

How to wire it, what each status means, and what each drum does.

Where this stands

Tested in Chrome on a Mac with a simulated audio input and a virtual MIDI port, using a synthetic drum loop. No real drum kit has been played through it yet, so the pad map and the audio estimates are starting points.

Wiring

This route is for a Pearl Mimic Pro and a Universal Audio Apollo Solo. Any interface and any kit with 5-pin or USB MIDI works the same way.

  1. Audio. Two 1/4-inch TRS cables from the Mimic Pro's Line Out 1 and 2, which carry the main mix, to the Apollo Solo's rear Mic/Line 1 and 2. Set both inputs to Line, and leave the front Hi-Z jack empty, because a plug there takes over input 1.
  2. MIDI. The Apollo Solo has no MIDI ports, and the Mimic Pro's USB slot is for installing samples. Run a 5-pin cable from the module's MIDI OUT into a USB-MIDI interface, and plug that into the computer.
  3. In the page. Press Connect, then Connect audio and MIDI, and allow both prompts. Choose "Universal Audio Thunderbolt" for audio. The MIDI list shows your interface's name, not the module's.
  4. Listening. These pages never play your input back, so keep monitoring through your interface.

Permission deniedThe browser or the system refused. The Connect sheet says which and how to allow it.
No deviceThe browser allowed it, and the computer shows no such input.
No signalThe device is open and nothing is arriving. Check the cable, the module's volume and the input gain.
LiveSignal is arriving. The label says whether hits come from MIDI or are estimated from audio.
DemoA synthetic drum machine, not your kit. The label stays up as long as it runs.
ReplayA session file you loaded. Nothing in it is live.

MIDI and audio together

MIDI names the drum, its velocity, the hi-hat pedal and cymbal chokes. Audio gives the picture its real spectrum. When both are live, MIDI decides the hits, so nothing fires twice.

With audio alone, four frequency bands estimate kick, snare, hat, tom and crash by a stated rule, and a ride is not told apart. With MIDI alone, each hit paints a stated spectrum shape rather than a measured one.

The note numbers on your module can be changed, so the map is a starting point. Open Connect, then Pad map and Learn, press Learn beside a pad and hit it.

Chimera Synth

Chimera is the one page here that makes sound. A MIDI keyboard or the computer keys play it, and it never listens to an audio input. Tested in Chrome on a Mac with a virtual MIDI port. No real keyboard has played it yet.

  • Connecting. A USB MIDI keyboard plugs straight in, and a keyboard with a 5-pin MIDI OUT needs a USB-MIDI interface. The sound plays through the computer's output.
  • Notes. Middle C is the root, and every channel plays the same way. From the computer, Z to M and Q to U play two octaves.
  • Status. Sound reads off, ready, playing, or paused by the browser, which starts sound only after a click. MIDI reads not connected, waiting, permission denied, no device, open with no notes, live, or disconnected. The demo says the page is playing itself.
  • Files. Samples are decoded in the browser. Chrome and Firefox open WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC and Ogg but not AIFF or CAF, so convert those first. Safari opens all of them, but has no Web MIDI.

Safety and privacy

A limiter slows any large change in brightness, and these pages measure under the WCAG limit of three flashes a second, even with hard playing. That is a measured screen, not a medical guarantee. If flashing or moving light affects you, take care, and turn on your system's reduced-motion setting, which tightens the limiter, calms the camera and removes the flash.

Audio and MIDI are analyzed in this browser and never sent anywhere. A session file holds hits, pedal moves and levels, with no audio in it.
